丁志明講座教授

Chair Professor Jyh-Ming Ting

國立成功大學材料科學與工程學系

Advancing AEMWE Technology: From Catalysts to Applications

講者簡介與演講摘要

講者介紹

Prof. Jyh-Ming Ting is a Chair Professor in the Department of Materials Science and Engineering at National Cheng Kung University (NCKU), Taiwan. He received his B.S. degree in Nuclear Engineering from National Tsing Hua University and his M.S. and Ph.D. degrees in Materials Science and Engineering from the University of Cincinnati, USA. His research interests encompass hydrogen energy materials, high-entropy materials, supercapacitors, lithium-ion batteries, thin-film materials, thermal management materials, and carbon nanotubes, with recent emphasis on high-entropy materials for electrocatalysis and electrochemical hydrogen production.

Prof. Ting has made significant contributions to the development of high-entropy electrocatalysts for sustainable energy conversion. His recent research has advanced high-performance and corrosion-resistant electrocatalysts for anion exchange membrane water electrolysis (AEMWE), including direct seawater electrolysis, demonstrating their potential for efficient and durable green hydrogen production. In recognition of his research achievements, Prof. Ting received the Outstanding Research Award from the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC), Taiwan.

演講摘要

Anion exchange membrane water electrolysis (AEMWE) has emerged as one of the most promising technologies for sustainable hydrogen production by combining the advantages of alkaline electrolysis with membrane-based architectures. Significant progress has been achieved in recent years; however, further advances in catalyst activity, membrane electrode assembly (MEA) engineering, system integration, durability, and scalability are still required before widespread commercialization can be realized. This plenary lecture will provide an overview of the current status of AEMWE technology and discuss the key scientific and technological challenges that govern its future development. Particular emphasis will be placed on recent advances in electrocatalysts for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER), highlighting rational catalyst design and the translation of materials innovations into high-performance MEAs and AEMWE systems. Representative examples will be presented to illustrate how improvements in catalyst design, electrode architecture, and device engineering collectively enhance electrolyzer efficiency and durability. Beyond conventional water electrolysis, the lecture will also discuss emerging applications of AEMWE, including coupling hydrogen production with value-added electrochemical reactions to improve overall energy efficiency and process economics. Finally, future research directions and opportunities for accelerating the development of AEMWE technology from fundamental materials research to practical applications will be outlined.

10:40 – 11:10｜IB-101

Dr. Hermann Tempel

Forschungszentrum Jülich, Germany
德國尤利希研究中心

Water electrolysis – challenges for the energy transition

講者簡介與演講摘要

講者介紹

Dr. Hermann Tempel is the Acting Department Head of Functional Materials and Components at the Institute of Energy Technologies (IET), Forschungszentrum Jülich, Germany. He is affiliated with the Fundamental Electrochemistry (IET-1) division, where his research focuses on electrochemical materials and technologies for sustainable energy storage and conversion. His research interests encompass alkaline water electrolysis, wastewater electrolysis, CO₂ electrolysis, solid-state batteries, post-lithium batteries, sodium-ion batteries, and aluminium-ion batteries.

Dr. Tempel’s research combines fundamental electrochemistry, functional materials, and advanced electrochemical characterization to develop and understand next-generation energy technologies. In the field of hydrogen production, he has been actively involved in the development of advanced materials, electrodes, and electrochemical systems for alkaline water electrolysis. His research also addresses process-water and seawater electrolysis for sustainable green hydrogen production, bridging fundamental electrochemical understanding with practical technologies for renewable energy conversion.

演講摘要

A substantial amount of literature exists on the subject of water electrolysis. However, the integration of this technology into the energy transition has fallen short of its expectations. This talk will provide an overview of the interrelations between the electricity market and hydrogen storage and usage.

For example, in Germany, a nation that has experienced significant renewable energy production penetration, the surplus of electricity from renewables induces negative electricity prices for over 1,000 hours in 2025 - highlighting the need for fast electrolysis deployment. However, at the same time, electricity prices over 100€/MWh omit economical hydrogen production for over 3,000 hours. Thus, integration of renewables requires the use of electrolyzers that can operate dynamically with long standby periods.

The conventional objectives for electrolyzers, such as efficiency, pressurized operation, and long durability, are being reevaluated in the context of the challenges posed by insufficient and scarce renewable energy penetration. The focus is shifting towards systems that are both cost-effective and durable, yet dynamic.

Dynamic operation of electrolyzers facilitates degradation, thus, necessitates understanding its effects on local process conditions to improve stability. In this regard, a significant hurdle to overcome is predicting local pH and ion concentrations over time. Using a custom-made optical electrochemical reactor and literature data for validation, we show that electrolyte property parameterization using modern electrolyte theory can significantly improve transport predictions in concentrated electrolytes. Thus, these models depict the basis for modelling dynamic operation in alkaline (membrane) electrolysis where cheap materials offer economic advantages compared to PEM electrolysis.

Finally, it is obvious that new emerging materials and concepts are required to solve the water-energy nexus. Which is the key to a widespread production of green hydrogen at economical prices.

11:20 – 11:50｜IB-101

管衍德終身特聘教授兼工程學院院長

Prof. Yean-Der Kuan

國立勤益科技大學冷凍空調與能源系

燃料電池系統設計製作暨 AI 整合應用

講者簡介與演講摘要

講者介紹

Prof. Yean-Der Kuan is the Dean of the College of Engineering and a Lifetime Distinguished Professor in the Department of Refrigeration, Air-Conditioning and Energy Engineering at National Chin-Yi University of Technology (NCUT), Taiwan. He received his B.S. degree in Aerospace Engineering from Tamkang University, his M.S. degree in Aerospace Engineering from the Georgia Institute of Technology, USA, and his Ph.D. degree in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering from the University of Missouri–Columbia, USA. His research interests include hydrogen energy and fuel cells, thermal management, refrigeration and air-conditioning systems, thermal-fluid analysis, energy-saving technologies, wind power generation, and microelectromechanical systems.

Prof. Kuan’s research integrates thermal-fluid engineering with sustainable energy technologies, with particular emphasis on system performance, energy efficiency, and practical engineering applications. In addition to his academic and research activities, he has been actively involved in professional societies related to hydrogen energy, fuel cells, and energy engineering. He currently serves as President of the Taiwan Society of Heating, Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning and has long contributed to the Taiwan Hydrogen and Fuel Cell Society and the Taiwan Energy Association, promoting research collaboration and the development of sustainable energy technologies in Taiwan.

演講摘要

可移動式質子交換膜燃料電池 (PEMFC)，具有潔淨無汙染、操作溫度低、啟動快等優點，在系統應用時，穩定的 PEMFC 運作與電力輸出及氫氣安全，是相當重要的環節。本演講的主要內容，主要是分享研究團隊於上述主題相關的研究與經驗，包含 PEMFC 系統控制器設計與氫氣使用效率之提升、氫氣洩漏量測與移除、輕型燃料電池車之設計與製作及 AI 應用於 PEMFC 故障診斷與壽命預估等，期使相關人員做為未來研究開發的啟發與參考。
